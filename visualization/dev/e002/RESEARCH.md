# E-002 development reconstruction · November 15, 2026

This folder is a published **development preview**, not a finalized science visualization. The E-002 spreadsheet fields have not been edited. The browser retrieves checksum-pinned TLE files from Willa Madsen's existing published project; this site does not commit a new copy of the raw E-002 elements. The [machine-readable manifest](data/provenance.json) identifies each dataset, provider, immediate retrieval source, available epoch range, checksum, and reuse status.

The [primary playback view](index.html) starts at **December 7, 2019, 09:45–11:15 UTC** and advances at 90 simulated seconds per real second. It automatically moves to December 8, 10:05–11:35 UTC, then December 31, 18:00–19:30 UTC; it pauses at the final chapter's end. No trajectory is drawn across the gaps. The first two chapter changes use a brief scene fade. The shared full visualization offers the same speed choices as E-001; its chapter playback begins at 1 simulated minute per real second so the 90-minute interval is navigable. Each spacecraft uses one dated TLE throughout a window. The [miniature table view](mini.html) uses **December 8, 10:05–10:50 UTC**, loops forward, and starts from the user's “This one please” saved view in [camera-examples.json](camera-examples.json). These are element-coverage windows, **not precise event timestamps**. The [seven-date candidate analysis](CHAPTER_ANALYSIS.md) compares these with Willa's original chapters without changing the public chapter sequence.

The published Episodes sheet selects plotted spacecraft on each page load. Only a complete, clean COSPAR identifier in Object A, B, or C is accepted; text containing a historical identifier does not select an object. As of this review, Object C is prose, so **only Kosmos 2542 (gold) and USA 245 (light gray) are loaded** in both full and miniature views. The previously configured 2543 remains available in reviewed metadata if a future published sheet again selects its exact identifier. USA 245 is omitted if an element lies outside the 36-hour display gate; pair statistics require both selected elements within 12 hours of their epochs.

The automatic camera stays on a 5.55-Earth-radius sphere with a 35° field of view. It aims near the midpoint of USA 245 and the selected Russian craft, preferring a direction with an unobstructed view of both. Camera orientation follows the selected orbital plane. The miniature uses a **co-moving radial/along-track/normal (RTN) frame**, centered on the pair, while Earth retains its actual Earth-centered orientation. Historical trails are sampled at 30-second spacing from fixed elements, allocated once, and revealed only behind the current time. The saved view is shifted in the viewport so both markers remain above the source cards and to the right of the expanded description. One-minute sampling at all 46 timestamps, at 1280 px, 760 px, and 390 px page widths, found both markers inside the visible preview, in front of Earth, clear of source cards, and visually distinct. The camera reduces its zoom in narrow layouts to fit the same orbital geometry. This is a screen-space visibility check, not an orbital accuracy claim.

The [shared E-002 route](../../Viz/compare.html?episode=E-002) uses E-001's page shell, satellite disclosures, chart slots, threshold controls, playback dock, and screen-label style. A single A–B separation curve and relative-speed curve show one-minute samples inside the selected chapter, with unsupported samples absent. Minimum values link to their exact sampled playback time. Dwell Time adds the intervals between consecutive supported one-minute samples where **both** user-adjusted thresholds are met at both endpoints; it is a conservative approximate chapter duration, not an episode-wide dwell. The optional `&dev=1` camera overlay retains saved-view tools. E-001 and E-002 still have separate 3D controllers behind the shared interface.

## Episode display copy

**Title:** Russia's *Kosmos 2542* and *Kosmos 2543* approaching U.S. reconnaissance satellite

**Description:** After launching *Kosmos 2542* in November 2019, Russia deployed a smaller satellite, *Kosmos 2543*, from it. The Russian spacecraft subsequently conducted proximity operations near *USA 245*, an American reconnaissance satellite, prompting public concern from U.S. officials in February 2020. Russia described the spacecraft as part of an inspection mission. Publicly reconstructed orbital data provide an opportunity to examine these encounters, although sparse observations of *USA 245* complicate precise assessments of separation and relative motion.

All epochs are element-set epochs, **not observation timestamps** and not proof of continuous coverage. The display gate is 36 hours from an epoch; the stricter numeric-pair gate is 12 hours for each craft. Neither is an uncertainty bound. A maneuver between an epoch and view time can invalidate even a short propagation. A distance computed from two TLEs is a model estimate, not a measured miss distance. The explorer withholds the apparent kilometer-scale A–C result near deployment, when public TLE errors and deployment dynamics could dominate it.

## Historical reading

- The [2024 GMV AMOS paper, section 2.1.4](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2024/Poster/Serrano.pdf) dates the Kosmos 2542 launch to November 25, 2019 and Kosmos 2543 release to December 6. It reports a 46 km minimum for early 2542–USA 245 passes, around 37.1 km for the first 2543 phase, 15–25 km for a later 2543 phase, and 150–300 km for 2542's January 22–February 1 pseudo-constant-proximity phase. These are **the authors' reconstructed findings**, not independently reproduced values here. The paper expressly notes limited USA 245 TLE availability. Its assertions about mission intent are analytical interpretation, not directly observed intent.
- [Nico Janssen's January 29, 2020 SeeSat-L message](https://www.satobs.org/seesat/Jan-2020/0198.html) describes 2542's raised orbit, USA 245's changed orbit, and near-aligned planes; it quotes an earlier December 31 amateur USA 245 element. [Michael Thompson's January 29 analysis](https://www.satobs.org/seesat/Jan-2020/0203.html) describes a 150–500 km trailing geometry after January 21–24 maneuvers. These are contemporary observer analyses, not official measurements. The original posts contain orbital parameters but no usable January USA 245 TLE in the local library.
- The linked episode records preserve [Raymond's February 10 interview](https://time.com/5779315/russian-spacecraft-spy-satellite-space-force/) as a U.S. official expression of concern, and a [Russian Foreign Ministry position published February 18](https://vpk.name/news/376303_v_mid_otvetili_na_obvineniya_ssha_v_presledovanii_amerikanskogo_sputnika.html) saying its spacecraft did not threaten the U.S. object. These are official positions, not orbital measurements.
- [U.S. Space Command's July 23 statement](https://www.spacecom.mil/Newsroom/News/Article-Display/Article/2285098/russia-conducts-space-based-anti-satellite-weapons-test/) says Kosmos 2543 released another object July 15 and links the system to the earlier U.S. satellite encounter. This is later context, outside the sheet's December–February E-002 scope; the prototype does not model July.

## Statistics and presentation recommendation

Kosmos 2542 is the defensible current public focus. GMV reports closer 2543 passes (about 15–25 km) but also reports repeated 2542 approaches around 46–70 km and a sustained 150–300 km phase in late January. Those are **GMV estimates**, not values reproduced by this site. The local A and C libraries each contain 55 Russian TLEs, so neither has a coverage advantage; the three USA 245 observer elements are the limiting data. The December 7, 8, and 31 windows support a cautious 2542–USA 245 demonstration, while the reported January proximity and the claimed historical minima cannot be reconstructed here. This recommendation also matches the live sheet's current A/B selection. New dated USA 245 elements would be needed before recommending an additional public chapter or a comparison of claimed close passes.

## GMV source audit and model comparison

The [2024 GMV paper](https://amostech.com/TechnicalPapers/2024/Poster/Serrano.pdf) acknowledges U.S. Strategic Command/Space-Track for orbit data, but does not print the exact USA 245 TLE lines, their epochs, a download date, or a machine-readable supplement. The [AMOS paper listing](https://amostech.space/year/2024/monitoring-of-rendezvous-proximity-operations-with-sst-and-sda-techniques-combination/) likewise links the paper without a data supplement. Therefore, the paper's element inputs cannot presently be reproduced or compared element by element. Its reported 46 km, 37.1 km, 15–25 km, and 150–300 km values should be labeled as **GMV's findings** when cited, never silently imported as local calculations.

The local B library has only the December 7, December 8, and December 31 element epochs during this historical episode, and no January or February 2020 B set. The early December and December 31 frames can show model geometry at selected times; none of those selected times is established as the GMV minimum. The 12-hour numeric gate rejects December 9 and January 1 B-pair statistics. The unsupported January 24 chapter has been removed from active playback. The local reconstruction cannot test the paper's January/February B-pair claims or its claimed minima. GMV's pair-specific relative-motion figures and phase annotations are useful design references for a future pair selector and maneuver-aware timeline, provided their published values remain source-attributed overlays rather than recomputed statistics.
